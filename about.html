The Workroom

Behind the Seams

Craftsmanship at Fairlawn

Step past the front counter and you'll find a workroom built for one thing: getting every stitch right. Every piece begins the same way — a conversation, a length of fabric, and a good look at what the garment is meant to do for the person wearing it. Measurements are taken by hand, not guessed from a size chart, and adjustments are made stitch by stitch until the fit is right.

Fabric and thread are chosen with an eye toward how a piece will wear over the years, not just how it looks on the first day out. Older techniques — hand-finished seams, pressed hems, considered interfacing — are kept alive here because they are, quite simply, what makes a garment last.

A Bit of History
National Register of Historic Places

Wauregan Historic District, Windham County. Roughly bounded by CT-12, CT-205, Third St, the Quinebaug River, and Chestnut St, Plainfield (900 acres, 113 buildings, 1 structure). Architectural style: Greek Revival, Late Victorian. Period of significance: 1850–1924.

All the housing in the village was constructed in the nineteenth century by Wauregan Mills for its workers, or by the mill owners for themselves. The largest homes were the residences of the Atwoods — the most impressive once stood on the east side of Route 12, commanding a full view of the mill and village below, built by John Walter Atwood (1880–1892), whose twin brother James Arthur Atwood built a similar house next door. Both homes were destroyed by fire in the late 1970s.

Behind the mansion stands a large carriage house that still exists today — the only visual reminder of the two grand houses that once stood on this hill.
